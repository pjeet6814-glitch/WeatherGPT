Before, during and after severe weather

General guidance for the most common weather dangers in India. Your state disaster management authority's instructions always come first.

In an emergency, call 112. Do not wait for an alert to reach you if you feel in danger.

Heavy rain and floods

Flood water can rise fast and hides open drains, potholes and live wires.

Do

  • Charge phones and power banks when a warning is issued.
  • Move papers and valuables to a high shelf or a waterproof bag.
  • Leave early for higher ground or an upper floor if authorities ask you to.
  • Boil or treat drinking water after floods.

Avoid

  • Walking, swimming or driving through flood water.
  • Touching electrical items or fallen wires when wet.
  • Staying in a basement as water rises.
  • Going near swollen streams, drains and low bridges.

Lightning

If you can hear thunder, you are close enough to be struck.

Do

  • Go inside a building or a vehicle with a solid roof.
  • Wait about 30 minutes after the last thunder before going out.
  • Stay away from windows during the storm.

Avoid

  • Open fields, hilltops and single tall trees.
  • Water, including pools, rivers and boats.
  • Metal fences and poles.
  • Plumbing and wired devices during the storm.

Heat

Children, older people, outdoor workers and people with health conditions are most at risk.

Do

  • Drink water often, even if you do not feel thirsty. ORS, buttermilk and lemon water help.
  • Stay out of the midday sun and cover your head outdoors.
  • Wear light, loose cotton clothes.
  • Check on neighbours who live alone.

Avoid, and watch for heat stroke

  • Hard outdoor work at midday.
  • Leaving children or pets in a parked vehicle.
  • Alcohol and very sugary drinks.

Very hot skin, confusion or fainting can mean heat stroke. Call 112, move the person to shade and cool them with wet cloths.

Cyclones

Follow evacuation orders as soon as they are given. Roads can close quickly.

Do

  • Keep your emergency kit and phone chargers ready.
  • Bring in or tie down loose items outside.
  • Stay indoors, away from windows, once the storm arrives.
  • Return home only when authorities say it is safe to do so.
  • Fishing communities: do not go to sea while a warning is in force.

Avoid

  • Going out during a sudden calm. It may be the eye of the storm, and strong winds return.
  • Ignoring an evacuation order because the weather looks calm.
