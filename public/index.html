How it stays honest

Weather advice can hurt people when it sounds surer than it is. These rules are built into every answer.

The assistant always

  • Shows official alerts in their own block, word for word.
  • Uses only numbers that appear in the live forecast.
  • Says when an alert stops being valid.
  • Tells you when data is missing or out of date.

The assistant never

  • Says something is "safe" or carries "no risk".
  • Guesses at flood risk or rainfall it has no data for.
  • Hides a day with heavy rain expected.
  • Treats "no alerts" as "no danger".

Try a question

  • Will it rain today?
  • Are there any official warnings for my area?
  • Should I carry an umbrella tomorrow?
